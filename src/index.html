    How it works

    A sensor sends a short ultrasonic ping. Sound spreads out in its beam, bounces off surfaces and loses energy with distance, in the air, and at every bounce. The sensor listens for the first echo louder than its threshold and turns the echo time into a distance: distance = c × t / 2.

    Try this

    • Rotate the wall a few degrees at a time and watch the echo drop out once the mirror bounce misses the sensor.
    • Swap concrete for acoustic foam and see the echo sink under the threshold.
    • Change the temperature: the real echo time changes, but the sensor still assumes 20 °C.
    • Load Crosstalk: two sensors, turn on Loop and watch false echoes from Sensor B creep closer every ping. Give both the same interval with offsets 30 ms apart and the problem goes away.

    Model and limits

    Geometric ray tracing, so no diffraction and no phase interference. Echo levels are in dB SPL at the sensor. Other sensors and interference sources are filtered by the active sensor's frequency band and timed against its pings.
